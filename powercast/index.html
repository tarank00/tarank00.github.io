README.TXT — how it works 
THE DATA
  Daily AESO pool price and Calgary/Edmonton temperatures.

  Sources:
   - Power prices: AESO API (Pool Price Report)
   - Weather: Open-Meteo historical weather API
     (Zippenfenig, P. 2023, doi:10.5281/zenodo.7970649),
     licensed under CC BY 4.0

  The model uses two things:
   - the day-to-day % change in power price
   - how far each day's temperature was from normal
     for that date

THE MODEL
  A separate t-copula for each season. A copula measures
  how two things move together without assuming a shape
  for either one. The t version allows both to hit
  extremes at the same time.

  You pick a temperature. The calculator finds where it
  ranks against past days, draws 20,000 matching price
  changes from the copula, and applies them to today's
  price.

WHAT I FOUND
  - Cold raises prices in winter; heat raises them in
    summer. Fall shows almost no link.
  - Weather moves the typical price much more than it
    causes extreme spikes. Big spikes mostly come from
    the supply side: low wind, plant outages, imports.
  - U.S. gas prices (Henry Hub) did not explain Alberta
    power prices. Alberta gas (AECO) often trades on its
    own because of pipeline limits.

LIMITS
  - Daily averages hide short spikes within the day.
  - Temperatures beyond the historical range are
    estimates, not data.
  - The gas price you enter only affects the profit
    figure. It does not change the simulated power
    prices, even though in reality higher gas prices
    tend to push Alberta power prices up.
  - Prices are capped at Alberta's $999.99/MWh limit.
